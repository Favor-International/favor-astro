Week one · the story

From a Death Wish
to Forgiveness

She had already decided that a woman was going to die.

Her name is Madina. She grew up in Upper Nile State under a stepmother who mistreated her without mercy, a woman Madina believed practiced witchcraft. When her mother died and her father's home fell apart, Madina was certain she knew who to blame, and when her own marriage later broke, the bitterness hardened into a vow. She went to a witch doctor and paid him to have her stepmother killed. Her own father begged her not to. She would not listen.

Then a Portable Bible School and a Trauma Counseling class came to her village of Joda. Madina sat in those classes carrying a plan for murder in her heart, and as she heard how Jesus forgave the very people who wronged Him, the Word of God started to loosen a grip that revenge had held for years.

I never thought in my life that I would forgive my stepmother one day. But today my life is renewed. Madina · Joda, Upper Nile State

Today she and her stepmother live in peace. Madina now sits faithfully in those same classes, growing in the grace that saved her.

If Madina's story is enough for you, you do not have to keep reading.

Getting our missionaries there

Sent out two by two, just as Jesus did. Fuel into villages other people do not drive to, and a place to stay for the full two weeks.

Madina is one of thousands.

For more than twenty years, our field missionaries have been carrying trauma healing across Africa, into places most people will never see. Little villages of grass and tin at the end of roads no one else drives. Refugee settlements. Police barracks. Government offices. Wherever there are wounded hearts, they go.

Some of the people in these photos walked in barefoot. Some came in uniform. In 2022, we watched top generals of the South Sudanese army kneel with their hands lifted high to receive the same teaching Madina received. One of the nation’s vice presidents sent his entire executive staff through it.

We are all traumatized. A vice president of South Sudan

And when the country’s top police commissioner shared that most of South Sudan’s suicides come from inside his own police force, our missionaries began walking alongside the police and their chaplains, training them in trauma healing.

Trauma does not check what you are wearing. Neither does the love of Jesus. From the tin hut to the government office, our missionaries carry the same gentle message into every one of these rooms: you are not what happened to you. You are a beloved child of God.

One person a month. Twelve a year. That is what steady giving provides that a single gift cannot: a next one.

Week three · the proof

Madina Was Not the Exception

A story can show you a moment.
It cannot show you whether the moment held.

A person can forgive on a Friday and carry it all back by Monday, and a class that changes one life in one village is a beautiful moment, not a ministry. So here is the room. Not a clinic. A patch of shade, some plastic chairs, and two of our missionaries who drove out to be there. Classes meet wherever people already are, because the people who need this most are the least able to travel to get it.

Grandmothers and teenage boys. Mothers with babies on their laps. Men who have never once been asked a question about their own life. What happened in these places did not happen only to women, and it did not happen only to adults, so healing cannot be offered only to some of them. Everybody who was there gets to come.

And here is a woman on the other side of years of this.

Her name is Dorcas. She was rescued out of human trafficking, and when she arrived she was carrying a child she did not yet know about. That is the part of a story people share. Here is the part almost nobody stays around long enough to see.

Dorcas is still with us. She and her daughter live at one of our children’s homes, and she runs the baking department there. The bread that feeds that home comes out of her hands every day. She is not a girl who was rescued. She is a woman other people now depend on.

Nothing about that happened quickly, and nothing about it started with bread. It started the way it always starts here, with somebody sitting down with her in what happened to her. Healing came first. The training, the work, the years, the daughter growing up watching her mother run a department, all of it walked through a door that trauma counseling opened.

Trauma healing is not one program among several. It is the first one, and everything else Favor does depends on it holding.

Madina, Dorcas, and every person on those benches are what happens when the same teaching keeps arriving, village after village, because somebody kept providing for it.

Week four · where it began

“I Did Not Come Here to Sow a Seed”

Twenty-four years ago I asked God to send me
where no one wants to go. He took me at my word.

When I first came, I did not have a program. I had a rented room, and a few people who met with me every morning to pray. What we found around us was a great deal of pain that no one had ever been able to say out loud. I told them what I had already told the Lord: I did not come here to sow a seed. I came to sow my life.

Then in 2004, God sent us a doctor. He was a believer from Australia, a physician who had been a missionary in Tanzania, and he had spent years learning what war does to a heart and how a heart heals. He had written a teaching for the children of that war: five steps, all built on forgiveness, every one of them from scripture, and written plainly enough that ordinary people could learn it without ever being licensed counselors.

So we gave him a week, and I sat through every day of it alongside everyone else. He walked a roomful of people through their weeping, and then through the pain underneath it, and then through the far harder work of setting down the anger they had carried toward one another for years. What waited on the other side of that was forgiveness. By the end of the week there was laughter in that room, and it was the first I had ever heard in that place.

By Friday we all knew, without anyone having to argue it, that this was what the nation needed. We held the first class that same year, in 2004. We have never stopped.

I could not have predicted what it opened. We have taught it to soldiers in uniform, in police barracks and government offices, in villages where revenge is planned three generations out. We have had two thousand people in a single class. In those two weeks, nearly everyone in the room gives their life to Christ. And then, almost every time, somebody asks the question I never get tired of hearing: “Do you have the next step?”

Trauma healing is the door. Everything else walks through it.

I am not asking you to fund an organization. Money has never been the point here. Every dollar is a soul. What I am asking for is consistency we can plan on.

Carole Ward
Favor International

Before you decide

Questions people actually ask.

Can I give a different amount than $50?

Yes. The levels are a starting point, not a rule. Choose the amount that fits your household. A $25 monthly gift that survives a hard month is worth more to this work than a $50 one that gets cancelled in March.

Does my gift only go to Trauma Counseling?

Favor Partner gifts are unrestricted, which lets our field leadership put them where the need is greatest that month. Trauma Counseling is what this campaign is funding and what steady giving keeps on the calendar, and it sits inside a wider work: Portable Bible Schools, Village Learning Centers, medical care, and women's empowerment.

What happens right after I give?

Your first gift processes immediately through the secure giving form and you receive a tax-deductible receipt by email. Then you hear from us: the stories, the prayer requests, and the names of the people your partnership is reaching.

Can I cancel or change it later?

Any time, for any reason, with no conversation required. Sign in to your Partner portal and manage it yourself, or reply to any receipt or call the office and a real person will handle it.

Is my gift tax-deductible?

Yes. Favor International is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit and an ECFA-accredited member. Audited statements and Form 990s are published on our accountability page.

Why monthly instead of one large gift?

Because a two-week class has to be provided for before it opens. A single gift funds a moment. A monthly gift is what lets our field leaders put the next village on the schedule and know it will still be provided for when the date arrives. That is the difference between a program that runs and a program that waits.
